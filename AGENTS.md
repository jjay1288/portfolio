# Portfolio editing rules

This repository is the public portfolio. Keep raw evidence, private resume exports, credentials, client identifiers, and internal planning outside it.

- Use Ronald Phillips as the public professional name. He was the sole responder for this incident, including containment and cleanup. Do not introduce separate responders from ambiguous labels in earlier notes.
- Write from evidence. Separate observations, source-reported results, hypotheses, and unknowns. Never invent personal actions, credentials, metrics, or outcomes.
- Preserve dates, accurate responder attribution, legitimate credits, and source history. Keep detailed correction and provenance records privately. Publish a coherent final case with a short edition date and evidence-based limits; retain source history in Git.
- Distinguish archived listings from live samples: the 1,095-byte archived child theme is not the 170,140-byte live malware sample. Keep initial-access uncertainty explicit. Negative signature searches do not establish that a system is clean.
- Write plainly. Remove copied assistant introductions, tool citations, generic praise, repetitive summaries, and unsupported certainty. Do not treat punctuation or an AI-detector score as proof of authorship.
- Do not use em dashes in newly written or revised portfolio prose. Use periods, commas, colons, or parentheses. Preserve literal evidence and code.
- Use direct destination links without unnecessary referral or campaign-tracking parameters. Preserve parameters needed for functionality or an explicitly quoted artifact; keep original evidence unchanged.
- Keep code, quoted artifacts, and indicators unchanged during prose edits unless a separate technical correction is justified. Do not run remediation scripts, execute payloads, contact attacker infrastructure, or probe client systems.
- Check local links, the diff, accidental private data, and factual consistency before committing. Read the exact staged changes before publishing. A content check is not proof of redaction or factual accuracy.
- Preserve unrelated work. Do not delegate unless the owner explicitly requests it.
